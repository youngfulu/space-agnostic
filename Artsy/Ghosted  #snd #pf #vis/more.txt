Acoustic and Atmospheric Memory

The text posits that sound is a primary vehicle for archiving experience. Forensic investigators use 'earwitness' interviews to recover the acoustic memories of former detainees to reconstruct the architecture of their confinement. Charles Babbage described the air as a 'vast library' where every word ever whispered is written as vibrations that never truly die. Music functions as a repository for memory, helping individuals find their way in the world, resurfacing repeatedly like a refrain. 'Sonic Meditations' function as auditory fantasy practices, encouraging the active imagination and sharing of remembered sounds through structured prompts.



In a contemporary world where memory is increasingly delegated to digital systems and machine infrastructures controlled by corporations, states, and private interests the project examines how the act of remembering is shaped, altered, and instrumentalized. Memory here is no longer passive recollection, but an active process of selection, distortion, and
reconstruction. Beyond its political and technological dimensions, Extractive Memories addresses memory on a deeply personal level. Drawing from lived experiences of traumas, and psychological rupture, the project explores the fragility and plasticity of recollection. Traumatic events leave vivid imprints in the internal archive, yet over time these images begin to fracture, overlap, and mutate -raising the question of whether what we
remember can still be trusted as reality. The project unfolds across multiple artistic forms spatial installations, video, sound, text, and live performance using documentary material intertwined with speculative narration. Through this hybrid approach, Extractive Memories investigates how personal memory, collective history, and technological mediation intertwine, producing layered realities where
fact and fiction blur. Rather than preserving memory, the project extracts it: isolating fragments, exposing distortions, and revealing the mechanisms- neurological, social, and digital through which memory is constructed. In doing so, Extractive Memoriesinvites audiences to reflect on authorship, control, and vulnerability in the ways we remember, forget, and reinvent
the past.
